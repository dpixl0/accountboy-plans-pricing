## The real cost of renewing, and the plans most people don't need

**Netflix at ¥29.8 per 26 days is not a monthly price.** The card runs 26 days, so a year of back-to-back renewals is 14 purchases and roughly **¥420**. Compare that against the restrictions before deciding: no TV app, five profiles per "car", and changing the password or PIN cancels your seat. If your main screen is a television, this is the wrong product regardless of price.

**ChatGPT Plus at ¥198 per 30 days is access, not ownership.** Twelve renewals come to about **¥2,376 a year**, and the product page states that discounted test accounts carry no after-sales cover on membership duration or on how long the account survives. Buy it to test a workflow, finish a deadline, or evaluate whether Plus is worth it. Don't build a daily production process on it and expect the login to be there in three months.

**Spotify's ¥299 is a renewal price, not a new-account price.** Two variants are sold: a ready Premium account, or a top-up applied to an account you already own. The 12-month renewal lands around $43–$52 depending on conversion, and because it's a manual top-up, once it's credited there's no refund path. Confirm the account details before paying, not after.

**The $3.32 Apple ID is the least risky purchase on the site** and also the most limited. It's for App Store downloads, not iCloud, and it's a one-time sale. Fine for grabbing a region-locked app; useless as a personal Apple account.

**Midjourney and the Gemini/Claude tier are where most casual buyers overspend.** If you make a handful of images a week, the Standard solo plan's 1,000 fast images plus unlimited relax is more capacity than you will use. The same logic applies to Pro-tier AI accounts bought for occasional use.

## After-sales: what the three-day window covers, and what it doesn't

Account and email products carry a **3-day after-sales window** from the purchase date, with free replacement for quality problems. That is the whole safety net. Everything else is carved out explicitly:

- **Card keys:** the platform states there is no after-sales period, though problems can still be raised with support.
- **Top-ups and recharges:** no cover once the credit lands. This covers Claude Pro direct top-ups and Spotify renewals.
- **Discounted AI accounts:** the membership duration itself isn't covered, and the verification window on these is shorter than the standard 3 days. Treat the clock as starting the moment the credentials arrive, not the next morning.
- **Claude:** the product page states that bans affecting China-region users have been frequent recently and are not handled.
- **Netflix:** changing the PIN or password voids the seat.

Delivery is fast when stock is in hand: the platform says more than 95% of orders ship automatically, the homepage advertises under five minutes, and the outside estimate is instant to 24 hours. The practical implication is that the 3-day window is mostly a window for *you* to verify, so log in the same hour, check that the plan tier matches what you paid for, and screenshot the order page and any chat. A replacement request filed on day three with no evidence is a hard conversation.

## What the third-party trust scores actually say

This is where a review has to be careful, because the numbers being circulated don't match the pages they're said to come from.

Scamadviser's page for the domain currently returns a **trust score of 0** and states the site "may be a scam", citing a Gridinsoft report that flags it as possibly harmful. Its only positive findings are a valid SSL certificate and a domain that has been registered for some years. ScamDoc, checking the same domain, gives it **86% ("Good", low risk)** on the strength of domain age and HTTPS, and notes that no user reviews have been left there. Two automated scanners, two opposite headlines.

Neither is a fraud verdict. Both are algorithms reading WHOIS records, certificate data and site patterns, and neither has ever bought an account. What they agree on is the factual base: HTTPS is valid, and the domain is not new.

The domain itself was registered on **13 May 2024** and renewed through May 2027, which puts it at roughly two and a half years old. That is a real operating history and short of the "many years" framing that promotional write-ups tend to use. It also creates an oddity worth knowing about: several testimonials displayed on the site are dated 2022 and 2023, before the current domain existed. That may reflect a predecessor domain or imported reviews, but as presented the dates don't line up, and it's a reason to weigh the on-site review wall (which the platform itself describes as 16K reviews at over 98% positive) as marketing rather than evidence.

Add the structural point that no amount of scoring changes: the products being sold are accounts that the platforms themselves can cancel.

1. **Land on the right domain.** The brand has published warnings about visual clone sites and phishing Telegram groups, and states that it does not run customer groups or take payments through private chats. If a "support agent" asks you to transfer money to a personal account, you are not talking to the seller.

3. **Change credentials immediately.** New password, 2FA on, recovery details updated. On shared Netflix seats, do the opposite: leave the password and PIN alone.
4. **Match the IP to the account's origin.** For purchased social accounts, use a residential proxy in the account's registered region, skip datacenter proxies, and warm the account up with normal browsing before running any campaign. One account per isolated browser profile if you're managing several.
5. **Check the terms on the specific page, not the category page.** Warranty length varies by product type, and the exceptions (top-ups, card keys, discounted AI accounts) are where most disputes start.

## FAQ

**Is buying these accounts legal?**
The platform-terms problem is real and documented. AccountBoy's own blog states plainly that buying or selling Facebook accounts violates Facebook's terms of service and can result in permanent suspension. That's a contract issue between you and the platform, and the consequence lands on the buyer. Whether reselling accounts is restricted where you live is a separate question worth checking locally.

**Will the account get banned?**
Some categories carry active enforcement. The Claude listing says China-region bans have been frequent and aren't covered; discounted ChatGPT Plus accounts carry no duration warranty; shared Netflix seats get revoked if you change the password. Email accounts and one-time region IDs are the steadier end of the catalogue.

**Can I get a refund?**
No refunds on delivered digital goods. What exists is a 3-day replacement window for quality issues on account and email products, and nothing on top-ups or card keys once credited.

**AI account or shared streaming seat, which is safer?**
The streaming seat. It's cheaper, the failure mode is a revoked slot rather than a locked account, and the rules are simple: don't change the password. AI accounts are the category where the vendor is actively hunting resold access.

**Is bulk pricing available?**
Yes. Quotes start at 10 units, and long-term business buyers can request custom pricing. New users also get a first-order discount at checkout. Confirm the quote in writing before paying.
